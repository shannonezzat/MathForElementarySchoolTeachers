Lemma 7.4.8 Euclid’s Lemma.

Let \(a_1,a_2, \ldots, a_k \in \mathbb{N}\) and \(p\) be a prime number. If \(p\) divides the product of our \(a\)’s; that is, \(p \mid a_1a_2 \ldots a_k\) then \(p \mid a_i\) for some \(1 \leq i \leq k\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Let’s first prove the result for a product of two numbers. Suppose that \(p \mid ab\text{,}\) where \(p\) is prime. If \(p \mid a\text{,}\) we are done. So let’s assume that \(p \nmid a\) and show that \(p \mid b\text{.}\)
🔗
Divide \(a\) by \(p\text{,}\) writing \(a=qp+r\text{,}\) where \(0 < r < p\text{.}\) The remainder cannot be \(0\text{,}\) since we assumed that \(p \nmid a\text{.}\) Multiplying by \(b\) gives \(ab=qpb+rb\text{.}\) We know that \(p\) divides both \(ab\) and \(qpb\text{,}\) so it also divides their difference \(rb\text{.}\)
🔗
Now divide \(p\) by \(r\text{,}\) then divide \(r\) by the new remainder, and continue in the same way. We can carry the divisibility statement along at every step. If \(x=qy+z\) and \(p\) divides both \(xb\) and \(yb\text{,}\) then \(p\) also divides \(zb=xb-q(yb)\text{.}\) Thus \(p\) divides each remainder multiplied by \(b\text{.}\)
🔗
The positive remainders get smaller at each step, so the process must end. Let \(d\) be the last nonzero remainder. Working backwards through the divisions shows that \(d\) divides both \(p\) and \(r\text{.}\) Since \(p\) is prime, its only positive factors are \(1\) and \(p\text{.}\) But \(d \leq r < p\text{,}\) so \(d\) cannot be \(p\text{.}\) Therefore \(d=1\text{.}\)
🔗
We showed that \(p\) divides every remainder multiplied by \(b\text{.}\) In particular, \(p \mid db\text{.}\) Since \(d=1\text{,}\) this says \(p \mid b\text{.}\) So, if a prime divides \(ab\text{,}\) it divides \(a\) or \(b\text{.}\)
🔗
Finally, suppose \(p \mid a_1a_2\cdots a_k\text{.}\) Apply the two-factor result to \(a_1\) and \(a_2\cdots a_k\text{.}\) Either \(p \mid a_1\text{,}\) or \(p\) divides the product of the remaining factors. In the second case, apply the same result again. Continuing in this way shows that \(p\) divides at least one of the \(a_i\text{.}\)
